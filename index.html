About Me

I'm an Erasmus Mundus scholar in the Copernicus Master in Digital Earth program, currently based in Strasbourg, France. My work sits at the intersection of satellite remote sensing, deep learning, and operational geospatial systems.

My master's thesis, hosted at ICube-SERTIT (University of Strasbourg) and jointly supervised by University of South Brittany and University of Salzburg, built generalizable wildfire delineation for rapid mapping across a heterogeneous ten-product, eight-sensor VHR archive — 100 scenes from 53 Copernicus EMS, International Charter and FireWatch activations, at 0.3–6 m resolution.

It moved from a single-sensor multi-task CNN to frozen geospatial foundation models — Panopticon, DINOv3 and DOFA — adapted with a sensor- and resolution-routed mixture of LoRA adapters, training 3.5 % of the model. The deployed system runs as an ONNX container on a single GPU.

Before that I worked on TomoSAR deep learning for forest canopy height, rice paddy detection in Thailand from Sentinel-2, and contributed to the VirtuGhan QGIS plugin. My background combines space navigation engineering from Egypt with European Earth Observation research.

From Single-Sensor Pipelines to Spectrally-Pretrained Foundation Models
Generalizable wildfire delineation from Very High-Resolution imagery for rapid mapping
ICube-SERTIT (Univ. Strasbourg) · Univ. South Brittany · Univ. Salzburg

Civil Protection needs burned-area maps within hours of a wildfire. Automating that is hard not because any one scene is difficult, but because the operational archive is heterogeneous: ten commercial product labels across eight sensor families, 0.3–6 m ground sampling distance, and at least four radiometric processing conventions with no vendor metadata to tell them apart.

The work adapts frozen geospatial foundation models with a sensor- and resolution-routed mixture of low-rank adapters, training 3.5 % of the model, and reports its negative results as carefully as its wins.

Selected findings

Finding 01
Preprocessing rivalled architecture

Dividing each band by its own per-scene 99th percentile beat per-sensor harmonization by 0.107 scene macro-F1 — needing no vendor metadata, and larger than the entire spread between spectral routes.

Finding 02
Pretraining beats access

An eleven-arm comparison held the near-infrared injection mechanism as the only variable. Spectral pretraining mattered more than spectral access — and the most theoretically principled injection route performed worst.

Finding 03
Where sensor conditioning belongs

Routing the low-rank adapters on acquisition metadata was worth three times an unrouted adapter of comparable size. Moving the same conditioning one layer earlier, into the frozen tokenizer, cost accuracy instead.

Negative result
Unsupervised adaptation did not transfer

Five adaptation strategies failed at VHR. Adversarial alignment was the clearest case: burn IoU 0.012 on the target region against 0.804 on its own source domain. It trained; it did not transfer. Labeled coverage recovered usable maps instead.

Methodological
Aggregation can flip the ranking

The common practice of dropping totally failed scenes from scene-level averages rewards a model for failing badly enough that its worst result stops counting — and reverses which method appears best.

Finding 04
Generalization is bounded by similarity

Across seven leave-one-region-out folds, transfer held across the Mediterranean (0.73–0.85) and collapsed outside it — bounded by ecological and radiometric similarity, not by spectral bands, model capacity, or data volume.
